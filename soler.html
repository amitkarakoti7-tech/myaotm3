What is solar energy?

Solar energy is the light and heat that arrive from the sun. Photovoltaic cells inside a solar panel convert light directly into electricity; solar thermal systems use heat for water, cooking, and industry.

Unlike coal or oil, sunlight does not need to be mined, shipped, or burned. It arrives every morning, quietly, wherever a panel can see the sky. A good solar system makes that daily rhythm useful without making the air dirtier.

The cleanest unit of energy is the one we never need to transport.
A short history

From a laboratory spark to a daily utility.

1839

The photovoltaic effect

French physicist Edmond Becquerel notices electricity flowing when light meets certain materials.

1883

First solar cell

Charles Fritts builds a selenium cell — an early, modest step toward a light-powered future.

1954

Silicon changes scale

Bell Labs demonstrates the first practical silicon solar cell, opening the door to modern panels.

Present day

Power for everyone

Solar is now one of the fastest-growing energy sources, from a village pump to a city rooftop.

Types of solar panels

There is no single best panel. The right choice depends on your roof, budget, weather, and how much energy you need.

Monocrystalline

20–23%

Made from a single silicon crystal. Sleek, space-efficient, and a strong fit for compact urban rooftops.

Best for: small roofs

Polycrystalline

15–18%

Built from multiple silicon fragments. A dependable, affordable option where space is less constrained.

Best for: value

Thin film

10–13%

Lightweight layers that perform well in diffuse light and on large, unconventional surfaces.

Best for: large spaces

A shared horizon

Why solar energy matters for our future.

India’s electricity story is also a climate story. Millions of families are gaining access to cooling, study time, irrigation, and reliable healthcare — and that demand will keep growing as the country develops. Solar lets that progress happen with fewer carbon emissions and less pressure on imported fuel.

It also changes who can participate. A school can make its own clean power. A farm can run a pump in a remote field. A housing society can turn an empty terrace into a shared asset. Small systems, connected thoughtfully, make the grid more flexible and communities more prepared for heat and extreme weather.

A brighter future is not somewhere far away. It is built roof by roof, field by field.

Rewa helped demonstrate that competitively priced solar could serve large public institutions and become a serious part of India’s energy mix.
